## The note

**Subject:** How should we spread these retention calls around?

Hi Vadim,

I want your thinking on something before I decide anything, and there's some background you won't
have seen yet.

**What we found.** We finally got the full shipment history out of the old system — every booking
back to 2022 — and it changed the picture. We'd been assuming our flat volume meant we weren't
winning enough new funeral homes. That turns out to be wrong. **We're winning more than ever. We
just lose them again.** Of the homes that shipped with us in 2023, close to half have placed no
booking at all this year.

The useful part is that it's a specific list of named funeral homes rather than a vague problem.
**About 400 accounts**, and the ones worth calling first are the ones still shipping but shipping
less — those are savable. Everything is ranked, with each home's actual booking history attached, so
whoever calls isn't going in cold.

**The question is who makes the calls.** Two ways I can see, and I'd like your read on both.

**Option 1 — give them all to Otto and Stacy.**

They have the capacity, and it's squarely their job. The problem is incentive: none of this counts
as new business, so none of it touches their commission. We'd be asking them to spend weeks on work
that doesn't pay them.

One possible fix: **treat any funeral home that hasn't shipped with us in two years as new
business**, regardless of territory. That's roughly what it is from their side — they'd be starting
a relationship from nothing. I'd need to work the details through with Donald and Cheryl.

**Option 2 — spread a portion around the office**, with a shot at a bump in bonus if a home they
call ships with us before year end.

This is just an idea at this stage and we'd need to work out the logistics and the financial side
with Donald and my mother before it's real. But it could give people a way to go above and beyond,
and it takes load off Otto and Stacy.

**Some accounts wouldn't go into that pool at all**, and I've made that a rule rather than a
judgement call each time.

**Any funeral home shipping around 50 or more a year comes off the rep lists entirely and sits with
you and my mother.** That's six accounts, and between them they're worth about 274 bookings a year
— roughly a fifth of everything at risk. Del Pueblo, Neshama and Levitt-Weinstein are the three
largest. The Ortiz homes and Foundation Partners sit in the same bucket.

**I want us being extra cautious with these.** They're big enough that a clumsy call costs more than
the call could possibly win, and they're relationships rather than accounts. **I'd rather you and my
mother manage that outreach between you** — and if you want to bring Otto or Stacy in on any of
them, that's entirely your call. I just don't want them going out cold to a customer at that scale.

**Which brings me to the part only you can answer.** Option 2 only works if people like Susej, Kim,
Renee or Abe can spare parts of their day for it. **I genuinely don't know whether they can, and
you do.**

My own worry is the 24/7 load. Outbound calling is a different kind of tiring from taking bookings,
and it competes for the same hours. If you think it adds pressure where we can't afford it, say so
and we'll find another way — I'd rather protect the desk than get a few more calls made.

**So: what do you reckon?** Option 1, option 2, some mix, or something I haven't thought of. And if
option 2 has any legs, you'd be the one deciding who takes what and how much — not me.

No rush. Have a think and let me know.

Thanks,

Ethan
