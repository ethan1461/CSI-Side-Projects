# The Gather question set — what to ask Chase Downs, in what order

**Status:** For Ethan. **He sends and says all of it** (`§7.1`). No agent contacts Gather
(`§7.5`).
**Supersedes** the single-ask version of this file.
**Read first:** `reports/gather-partnership-intel.md` — confidence LOW, and it says why.

---

## The shape of it

There are three different conversations here and running them in the wrong order costs CSI
money.

1. **Is it worth doing?** — sizing. Cheap, factual, goes in writing.
2. **How would it actually work?** — mechanics. Belongs on a call.
3. **What does CSI pay?** — economics. Last, and only once 1 and 2 are answered.

**The mistake to avoid is naming a number in conversation 1.** Gather asked for a "tangible
outcome," which creates pressure to put a percentage on the table early. A percentage before
the sizing is a guess (`§7.3`), and a guess you have said out loud is a floor you negotiate
down from.

---

## Round 1 — in writing, with the thread Kyri opens

Four questions. None requires them to share a customer list, none commits CSI to anything.

**1. What share of your customers' cases involve a transfer out of state or abroad — and
does Gather capture that as a field?**

**This is the question that decides everything else.** Five hundred funeral homes that only
bury locally are worth nothing to CSI; fifty with regular international volume are worth a
great deal. If the field exists, they can answer as an aggregate in a day. `market-intel`
could not establish whether it does.

**2. Roughly how does your customer base break down by state? Counts only.**

Tells you whether their base sits near CSI's eight gateways. Offer the same cut of CSI's in
return — state-level counts of funeral homes shipped for is business-level aggregate, no
names, `§7.2`-clean. **It is still CSI's distribution footprint**, so that offer is your
call; the ask works without it.

**3. When you say customers, is that firms or locations? Single-site independents or
multi-location groups?**

Their own pages say "500+ funeral homes" in one place and "2,000+ deathcare professionals"
in another. Nothing public supports the 800 Kyri heard. **Do not quote any count back to
them as fact, including their own.**

**4. Do your customers have a shipping or repatriation partner they're pointed to today?**

`market-intel` found no transport category on their integrations page, but could not read
the production page, so treat the absence as unconfirmed. If a partner exists, the whole
conversation changes and you want that now, not in February.

## Round 2 — on a call. How it would actually work

**5. How do your existing partnerships work commercially?**

**Ask this before CSI proposes anything.** They already run partnerships — accounting,
insurance, video, aftercare. Whatever structure they use is a template they have already
agreed to internally, and it is far easier to fit into than to invent one. It also tells you
what they consider normal, which is the single most useful thing you can learn cheaply.

**6. Where would CSI actually appear?**

This is the difference between a distribution channel and a logo swap. In-app, at the moment
a case needs transport? A directory page? A marketing email twice a year? **A referral that
arrives at the point of need is worth many times one that arrives in a newsletter**, and the
answer should drive what CSI is willing to pay.

**7. How do you reach your base, and what has actually worked for other partners?**

Email list size and engagement, in-app messaging, user conference, webinars. Ask for what
moved the needle for someone else rather than what is theoretically available.

**8. What would a first step look like for you?**

Lets them scope it. Also surfaces whether they want a pilot or a signed arrangement, which
tells you how much process is coming.

**9. What does good look like for you in twelve months?**

**The highest-information question in the set, and the one to listen hardest to.** If the
answer is revenue, this is a commercial negotiation. If it is *differentiation* — being the
platform that solves transport for its customers — **CSI can get a far better deal, because
what Gather wants is something CSI can supply without paying for it.** Kyri's read was that
CSI's service is unique enough that both sides genuinely gain; question 9 tests that.

---

## Round 3 — economics, once 1–9 are answered

**10. How would either of us know it worked?**

Not a soft question. **A revenue share CSI cannot attribute is unadministrable.** If a
funeral home hears about CSI through Gather and then phones the desk, nothing in CSI's system
records why. Attribution needs a source field the OMS does not have today, which makes it a
Monday engineering ask — before any percentage is agreed, not after. `CLAUDE.md §9.2`:
instrumentation precedes spend, and that rule is not waived for urgency.

**11. Would either side expect exclusivity?**

Worth real money in both directions and easy to give away by accident. If Gather would
decline to work with Eagle's Wings or Inman while CSI is in, that is a concession CSI should
pay for deliberately rather than receive by implication.

**12. Are you talking to other repatriation providers?**

Fair to ask directly. The answer changes CSI's leverage and they will likely tell you.

## Two things to listen for rather than ask

- **Whether they can build anything at all.** No public API surfaced, and funeralOne, Passare
  and ASD all have one. If Gather cannot integrate, "revenue share" quietly means
  "co-marketing with paperwork," and should be priced as co-marketing.
- **Funeral 365.** The brain records a warm CSI relationship with a direct Gather competitor.
  Expect it to come up, or to surface later and look like something CSI withheld. **How you
  handle that is yours and Cheryl's call** — saying nothing is a choice too, but it should be
  a chosen one.

---

## What not to say

- **No number, no percentage, no structure** until rounds 1 and 2 are answered.
- **No commitment language** — nothing that reads as an offer, an LOI, a term sheet or an
  agreement to agree (`§7.5`).
- **Not CSI's funeral-home count.** Kyri used ~1,000 on the call. That figure is entangled
  with the partner-routing correction — a material share of the homes in CSI's history arrive
  via NMS, IMS or Bergen and are not CSI's own customers. Use **~11,000 loved ones a year
  across eight gateways** (`CLAUDE.md §1`), which is established and lands harder.
- **No pricing specifics** about what CSI charges funeral homes. Not an antitrust issue —
  Gather is not a competitor — but it is a negotiating position, and rounds 1 and 2 do not
  need it.
