## The note

**Subject:** Your call list — 104 accounts, ranked

Otto,

Attached is your call list. Some background on where it came from, because it changes how to read it.

We finally got our full shipment history out of the old system — every booking since 2022. It told
us something we had wrong. We'd assumed our flat volume meant we weren't winning enough new funeral
homes. **We're winning plenty. We lose them again.** Of the homes that shipped with us in 2023,
close to half have placed no booking at all this year.

So this isn't a prospecting list. **It's a list of people who already know us.**

**You have 104 accounts to call**, in three groups:

- **16 at risk** — still shipping, but less. **Call these first.** They're savable, and together
  they're worth around 398 bookings a year
- **17 dormant** — stopped altogether. Worth knowing why
- **71 one-and-done** — shipped once and never came back

The list is in call order. Every row has that home's actual booking history and a reason to call, so
you're never opening cold. Your first three:

1. **Del Pueblo Funeral Home** (TX) — 139 bookings in 2024, 82 in 2025, 61 so far this year
2. **Neshama JFS LLC** (FL) — 113 in 2024, 137 in 2025, 57 this year
3. **Levitt-Weinstein Memorial Chapels** (FL) — 58 in 2024, 66 in 2025, **13 this year**

**Three things before you start:**

1. **21 rows at the bottom are marked HELD.** Those are locations inside a corporate group and we're
   waiting on a conversation at their head office. **Don't call them yet** — I'll tell you when
   they're clear. They're sorted last so you shouldn't hit them anyway.
2. **If a partner account appears, bring it to me.** Bergen, National Mortuary Shipping,
   International Mortuary Shipping and Las Rosas are customers *and* partners. They should be
   filtered out, but if one slipped through, stop.
3. **Phone numbers aren't on the sheet** — pull them from HubSpot or the OMS account page. That's
   deliberate; we keep contact data in one place.

**On the calls themselves.** These are funeral directors who used us and stopped, and most won't
remember deciding to. The honest opening works best: we noticed, we wanted to check nothing went
wrong. Not a win-back pitch. If they've moved to someone else, that's useful to know and worth
asking about plainly.

**The part I'd really like you to do properly** — there are four blank columns on the right:
outcome, a reason code, one sentence of what they said, and a next action.

**We have never once asked a funeral home why it stopped using us.** Not in 23 years. Everything we
think we know about why accounts leave is inference. Forty or fifty filled-in rows would be the
first real evidence we've ever had, and it will shape what we do next year far more than the calls
themselves will.

Start wherever you like, but the top of the at-risk list is where the volume is.

Shout if anything looks wrong — the list is a first version and you'll spot things I can't.

Ethan
