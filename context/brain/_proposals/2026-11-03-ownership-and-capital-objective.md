## The thing corpdev must test first, and early

**$25M for 51% implies a whole-enterprise value near $49M.** Against FY26 revenue tracking
to roughly $15.0M, that is about **3.3x revenue.**

**Logistics brokerages are not normally valued on revenue, they are valued on EBITDA**, and
typical multiples in this part of the market are a single-digit multiple of earnings. CSI's
EBITDA is **unknown** — `§4.3` — and cannot be computed until Arty confirms the basis of
accounting and how carrier cost is booked.

**No valuation figure is asserted here** (`§7.3`). What is asserted is that **the gap
between the owner's number and a market number has to be measured before anything else is
planned**, because one of three things is true and they lead to completely different years:

1. The number is achievable today → the work is packaging and process, and the timeline is
   driven by the 2027 succession date.
2. The number is achievable after the growth programme → **then the FY27 plan is not a
   growth plan, it is a value-creation plan with a target**, and every initiative should be
   judged on whether it closes that specific gap.
3. The number is not achievable on this trajectory → that is the most important thing
   anyone could tell the principal, and it should be said early and plainly rather than
   discovered during a process.

**This reframes the FY27 target conversation.** Ethan set +5% bookings on 2026-11-03 and
`sales-lead` then showed the bottom-up path reaches roughly 42% of it. **If the real
objective is closing a gap to $25M by 2027, bookings growth may be the wrong headline
metric** — margin, contracted revenue quality and customer diversification move an EBITDA
multiple more than volume does, and `CLAUDE.md §0` already names enterprise value as the
compounding scoreboard.

**That is a recommendation to put to Ethan, not a decision to take.** He ruled "volume
wins" on 2026-11-03 **before** stating the $25M objective, and he should be given the
chance to revisit it with the number in view.
