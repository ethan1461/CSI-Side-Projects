## Why I need it

Every published AI-roll-up screen assumes a large and **growing** labour market — General
Catalyst sizes US services at *"$6T+"*, and the fund-scale examples are $200bn+ categories
(`reports/ai-rollup-thesis-grounding.md` §1, §5). CSI's category is a fraction of that, and
Dewez's criterion *"large and fragmented market growing faster than GDP"* is the one criterion
the grounding report scores as an outright **Fails**.

`reports/ai-rollup-screen-and-platform-control.md` §D.4 item 4 makes this worse: the
category-growth failure and the "capacity-constrained workforce" strength are **not
independent**. Fig's mechanism is that released capacity taken as *growth* compounds — but
growth needs demand to absorb it. In a shrinking category, released capacity has nowhere to go
except share taken from competitors, or into adjacent lines that nobody has sized. So the
"somewhere obvious to go" is not obvious, and your answer decides whether it exists.
