## The answer

1. **Ethan's read is half right and the half that is wrong matters.** EWA did expand its international program and did add something called "Indirect Air Carrier capabilities." But an IAC is a TSA cargo-security approval. It is not consular, apostille or country-paperwork capability. **It does not, by itself, close CSI's differentiator #4.** `[INFERRED — regulatory definition of IAC; see section 3]`
2. **"They got their IAC" is not established.** The release, as summarized, says the appointee's company adds IAC capability to EWA's program. That reads as capability arriving through a partner forwarder (Indigo Logistics, which the new manager keeps leading), not as EWA itself holding a TSA approval. Nobody outside TSA can confirm either way: there is no public TSA IAC list `[INFERRED — one secondary source says TSA publishes none; not confirmed against TSA]`. `[UNKNOWN — who holds the IAC]`
3. **CSI does not hold an IAC either.** `context/brain/company-facts.md`: "CSI holds no TSA or IATA certification; affiliate Southern Cross Trading does." So CSI's international claim cannot rest on holding a certificate, and CSI should not make an IAC the terms of this comparison.
4. **What EWA gained that does matter:** per the release summary, "new international airline options and departure airports nationwide." That is a **routing and carrier-access** gain. It attacks differentiators #2 and #3 (carrier access, routing options) on international lanes more than it attacks #4 (documentation depth).
5. **The exposure hypothesis is only partly supported.** The "international carries the margin" premise rests on **four clean international lanes** at 41.2% (`reports/carrier-margin-resolved.md`, line 24, 85: "directional"). There is no cost data for 15 carriers covering 93% of the lane book. And corridor mix is unmeasurable (no shipment-level export). So CSI cannot currently say how much margin is actually at risk. **That gap is the real finding.** The risk is plausible; its size is unknown.
6. **Nearest-term consequence:** the sales objection "we're also talking to Eagle's Wings," now with an international angle. Draft line is in section 5 and the handoff file.

## 3. What this does and does not give them

An IAC is a TSA-approved security program for forwarders who tender cargo to airlines (49 CFR Part 1548). It lets a non-airline party consolidate and tender freight without every piece being screened by the airline. `[INFERRED — regulatory framework, search results]`

| Capability | Does an IAC (or Indigo partnership) give it? | Effect on CSI |
|---|---|---|
| Tender cargo to carriers directly, incl. international | **Yes, if held.** | Narrows the gap on carrier access |
| Carrier and airport options on international lanes | **Likely** (the release's claim) | Narrows differentiators #2, #3 |
| Consular documentation, apostilles, translations, country rules | **No.** Separate competency | **Not closed by this news.** `[UNKNOWN whether EWA has it; UNKNOWN whether it was ever weak]` |
| Overseas receiving-end coordination | **No** | Unchanged |
| 24/7 named live agents | **No** | Unchanged |

**Verdict: narrows differentiators #2 and #3 on international; leaves #4 untouched pending evidence.**

Two cautions on our own side, because the claim cuts both ways:
- **CSI's "competitors underperform" claim has no evidence in this repo.** I found no data on EWA's documentation performance, either way. We are defending a belief.
- CSI's documentation depth partly runs through its network: Bergen is the established partner for international documentation processing (`CLAUDE.md` §5). That is a strength and also a dependency. EWA could also buy this capability through partners.

## 4. Where CSI is most exposed

Corridor mix is **unmeasurable today** (`CLAUDE.md` §4.3), so this ranking is reasoning, not data. Labeled accordingly.

1. **Funeral homes that already use EWA domestically and have occasional international cases.** Lowest friction for EWA, and it is the exact "we already work with EWA" objection (`context/sales-playbook-v2-2026.md`). Most exposed. `[INFERRED]`
2. **Lanes where price is set by carrier access, not paperwork complexity.** EWA's gain is airlines and airports. Corridors served by many carriers and gateways (Europe, Mexico/LatAm via common hubs) are more contestable than corridors where consular complexity dominates (China, Israel). `[INFERRED]`
3. **Least exposed: enterprise groups on the portal path (SCI/Dignity pilot)** and corridor-specialist homes that buy documentation expertise (Funeraria del Angel, Groman Eden, King David, Green Street, Hawaii homes — the West Coast priority list in `accounts-and-pipeline.md`). `[INFERRED]`
