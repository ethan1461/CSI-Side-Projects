## What CSI can and cannot physically handle

- **CORRECTION — export-only for physical handling.** CSI physically handles shipments
  **leaving** the U.S., not imports. This applies only to physically handling human
  remains. Technology, referrals, and consular/document support apply worldwide.
- **Inbound (into-U.S.) model:** a licensed customs partner from CSI's network clears the
  remains at the arrival gateway; CSI then handles the domestic onward leg and final
  delivery anywhere in the U.S. For U.S.-bound imports CSI refers partners to its network
  (often partners at Bergen). The network is CSI's core strength.
- U.S. regulations bar CSI from issuing air waybills for shipments that do not touch the
  U.S. For non-U.S. lanes CSI can only help partners pick the right carrier.
- Any copy that says CSI itself clears customs on inbound shipments needs review against
  this before it goes out. Flag it; do not silently rewrite approved copy.

## Pricing and business model

- Funeral homes pay the **published airline rate with nothing added** — no booking fee.
- **Margin (data, 2026-11-02):** ~35% gross on contracted domestic lanes, ~41%
  international — the spread between published and contracted carrier rates. Quote only
  with the limits in `reports/carrier-margin-resolved.md` (rate-card margin, unweighted
  by volume, excludes cost to serve, no cost data for most international carriers).
- **Open conflict:** in October 2026 Ethan described CSI as earning on secondary services
  (consulate support, pickup/drop-off) rather than on the rate. The margin data says the
  rate spread is the main engine. Until Ethan rules (`open-questions.md` #7), agents cite
  the data and never describe the revenue model externally.
- Not a call center; named people on call 24/7/365.
- Working relationships with every major airline — the largest airline network in the
  space.
- **Never reveal the pricing model or margins to partners or prospects.**
