## The note

**Subject:** Would the desk have room for some retention calls? Genuine question

Hi Vadim,

Something I'd like your read on before I go any further with it, because it's your desk and you'll
see the problem with it faster than I will.

**The short version of what we found.** We finally got the shipment history pulled, going back to
2022, and it changed the picture. We've been assuming our flat volume was because we weren't winning
enough new funeral homes. It isn't — we're winning plenty, more than ever actually. The problem is
that we lose them again. Of the homes that shipped with us in 2023, a bit under half have placed no
booking at all this year.

The useful part is that it's a specific, named list rather than a vague problem. Around 400 funeral
homes, and the ones worth calling first are the ones still shipping but shipping less — those are
savable.

**What I'd like to understand is whether the desk has any room in it.** Otto and Stacy are taking
the bulk. But there's a tail of smaller accounts, and I wondered whether Susej, Renee or Abe might
pick up a slice — they know how a booking actually works, which on a call like this is worth more
than sales technique. The ask would be roughly **[N] calls each, spread over about six weeks**.

**I'm genuinely asking, not proposing.** Three honest options:

1. **No** — the desk is at capacity and this is the wrong thing to add. Completely fine, and I'd
   rather hear it now than find out in January.
2. **A capped slice** — a fixed number a week, in quiet hours, that you set rather than me.
3. **The full tail** — if there's more room than I think.

**My own worry is the 24/7 load.** Outbound calling is a different kind of tiring from taking
bookings, and it competes for the same hours. If you think it adds pressure where we can't afford
it, say so and we'll find another way — I'd rather protect the desk than get a few more calls made.

If it's a yes in some form, the lists are ready and I'd want you deciding who takes what, not me.

No rush on this — have a think and let me know what you reckon.

Thanks,

Ethan
