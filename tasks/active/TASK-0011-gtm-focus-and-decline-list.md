# TASK-0011 — Decide what CSI does not do this quarter, and name the one volume play worth a test

**Owner:** gtm-strategist
**Created:** 2026-11-02 by pm-orchestrator
**Due:** 2026-11-06
**Priority:** HIGH
**Status:** UNCLAIMED

## Objective
2026 growth is yield, not volume: revenue +4.4% on bookings +1.3% Jan–Aug
(`CLAUDE.md §2`). Volume growth of 1.3% does not make anyone the industry leader. The job is
converting a yield-led recovery into a volume-led one **without giving the yield back**.

CSI currently has more named growth initiatives than it has capacity: three FY26 pillars, two
new reps, a portal, an acquisition program, a capital raise, federal contracting, software
distribution partnerships, trade shows, white-glove/HNW, and 202 international partner
prospects. Eight weeks remain in FY26 and the team can start twelve tasks.

**Your first deliverable is the decline list.** With this much mandate and this little
capacity, the highest-value GTM output is focus. Then name the single volume play worth a test
this quarter and the smallest test that would change our mind about it.

## Line to the metric
Primary operating metric — profitable landed bookings per month. A decline list raises it by
protecting the few plays that can actually move it from the twenty that would dilute them.
North star — category leadership requires winning somewhere specific first.

## Deliverable
`reports/gtm-focus-and-decline-list.md`:

1. **What CSI does not do this quarter.** Every named initiative, each marked pursue / hold /
   decline, with a reason in one sentence and **what would change the call**. A hold with no
   promotion trigger is a decline pretending otherwise. Include the FY26 memo's Pillar B
   (software platform distribution — Afterword, Funeral365, FuneralOne, OneDirector — and
   government contracting), federal contracting, white-glove/HNW, trade shows, the 202
   international partners, and the enterprise group plays.
2. **The one play.** The single highest-expected-value source of incremental volume, with the
   addressable volume, the cost to pursue, the owner, and why it beats the runner-up.
3. **The smallest test.** Before anyone spends, what would $500 or one week tell us — and what
   result would kill the play. If the test cannot be measured today, say so and name the
   instrumentation; that is a `§9.2` blocker, not a detail.
4. **The yield-protection constraint.** State explicitly what the chosen play must not do to
   price or mix. A volume play that trades away the 3.2% yield gain is a bad trade and should
   be named as such.
5. **The ICP sharpening the data supports today.** International corridors carry higher
   measured margin than domestic (~41% vs ~35%, with that report's limits). Say whether that
   justifies prioritizing international-heavy independents, and on what evidence.
