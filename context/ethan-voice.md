# How Ethan writes

**Read this before drafting anything that goes out under Ethan's name.** Added 2026-11-25
at his instruction: *"Please start to take notice of my tone. This is how I speak."*

Built from emails he wrote himself and from the edits he made to drafts this team produced.
The edits are the more useful source, because they show what he consistently removes.

---

## What he cuts from our drafts, every time

This is the pattern. We add these; he deletes them.

| We write | He writes |
|---|---|
| "Apologies — you're right, I owed you that." | "Apologies — you're right." |
| "I should have had this before I asked you." | *(cut entirely)* |
| "If it would help, we can start capturing that going forward." | *(cut entirely)* |
| "Happy to recut either if you'd define them differently." | *(cut entirely)* |
| "Best," | "Thanks," |

**The rule behind it: he apologises once and briefly, and he does not stack offers at the
end of an email.** A draft that ends with three hedged offers to do more work reads as
anxious. He cuts to one ask or none.

## What he does that we under-do

- **He explains why he is asking**, not just what he wants. The reasoning comes before the
  request.
- **He gives people an explicit way out.** *"If you'd rather stay on the call lists, that's
  a perfectly good answer."* He means it, and the out is stated as plainly as the ask.
- **He names the trade-off honestly, including the cost to the other person.** *"The upside
  is a chance to nurture the relationship. The cost is that you'd need to stay on top of
  when these customers book."*
- **He says what he does not know.** *"We don't have a good sense of the volume yet."* No
  padding around it.
- **He writes in lists when there are more than two things.** He converted our prose into
  numbered lists more than once.

## Mechanics

- **Opening:** first name, comma. "Joe," or "Hey Stacy," for colleagues. Never "Dear",
  never "I hope this finds you well."
- **Closing:** "Thanks," — and his signature block follows. Not "Best regards."
- **Short paragraphs**, usually two to four sentences, with a blank line between.
- **Em-dashes for asides**, and he uses them often.
- **"We" for the company, "I" for his own view and his own mistakes.** He does not hide
  behind "we" when the thing was his.
- **Bold** for the sentence that matters in a long email. Sparingly.
- **No corporate filler.** No "circling back", no "per my last email", no "touching base",
  no "as discussed".

## Tone

Warm but not padded. Direct without being curt. He is comfortable saying a thing is not
good enough, and equally comfortable saying someone was right. He writes to people as
adults who can take a straight answer.

**On a difficult message** he leads with the substance rather than cushioning it, then
makes the path forward easy. He does not soften a fact; he softens what happens next.

## Where this does not apply

- **`§7.4` governs anything a grieving family might read.** This file is about Ethan's
  voice in business correspondence. External marketing copy follows §7.4 first.
- **`§7.5`** still bars binding language, securities language and any approach to a target,
  investor or lender, however it is phrased.
- **He sends everything.** This file makes drafts better; it does not make them sendable.

## Maintaining this

Every time he edits one of our drafts, the edit is evidence. If he removes something this
file does not predict, add it. **The test of this file is whether his edits get smaller.**
