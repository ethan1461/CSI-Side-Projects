## Notes for Ethan — delete before sending

**Why this meeting may matter more than it looks.** Capital is Priority 1, and the
first corpdev task is a valuation. **A valuation built on numbers that don't reconcile to
the financial statements is worth nothing** — worse than nothing, because it gets quoted
in a room where the assumptions aren't visible (`§7.3`). Four questions below decide
whether that work can even start honestly, and they're all Arty's:

1. **Cash or accrual basis.** If CSI's statements are cash-basis, every lender and every
   buyer will require an accrual conversion, and our margin analysis doesn't tie to the
   P&L at all. This is the single most consequential question in the note.
2. **What level of assurance exists** — compiled, reviewed, or audited. This sets the
   floor on what kind of capital is available and how long a raise takes. It is better to
   learn this now than in a diligence request.
3. **How carrier cost is booked.** We've been quoting ~35% domestic / ~41% international
   gross margin from rate cards. If carrier cost sits somewhere other than COGS, or is
   netted against revenue, those figures may not reconcile to the financials — and the
   first person to notice would be a lender.
4. **Entity structure.** Affects everything about how a raise or an acquisition is done.

**Three guardrails I've applied, and you should know why:**

- **No labour cost or payroll asks.** `§9.6`: anything touching labour cost routes
  through Donald. Arty may well hold payroll data, but asking him directly would route
  around that rule. If he turns out to be the source, loop Donald in rather than
  substituting.
- **No figures asked for on owner compensation.** The note asks *what would need
  normalizing* for a third party, not what anyone is paid. The actual numbers involve
  Cheryl and Donald personally and are your conversation, not an accountant's cold ask.
- **Nothing investor-facing, and no projections.** `§7.5`: the note asks only about
  historical records that already exist. **It contains no forward numbers at all**, and
  nothing in it reads as preparing an offering. Any projection that eventually goes to a
  lender needs counsel and Donald first.

**One decision that is yours: whether to tell him you're exploring a raise.**

I've written it in, in one sentence, because (a) it's the honest reason for the questions,
(b) he'll give better answers knowing the purpose, and (c) your own accountant finding out
later is worse. **But it's your call and it's a real one** — it is a confidence, it may
reach others, and §7.5 keeps deal specifics in the repo. The sentence is marked below so
you can cut it cleanly; the questions all stand without it. If you cut it, the honest
alternative framing is "getting our reporting in better shape," which is also true.

**If you only get through half the meeting:** questions 1, 2 and 4. They're yes/no, they
take ten minutes, and they determine whether the rest is worth doing.

## The note

**Subject:** A few things ahead of Thursday — mostly "what do we already have?"

Hi Arty,

Looking forward to sitting down this week. Most of what I want to cover is just
establishing what we already have, so I thought I'd send it ahead rather than work it out
in the room.

Some background on why: we're trying to turn Cargo Sales into a genuinely data-driven
business, and I've found we can't currently answer some fairly basic questions about our
own economics. I can tell you what we charge on 6,900 routes and almost nothing about what
they cost us. I'd like to fix that, and most of the foundation sits with you.

*[**Optional sentence — Ethan's call, see notes:** We're also starting to look seriously
at raising capital and at acquiring smaller operators in our space, which means our
financials are going to get read by people outside the company for the first time. I'd
rather find out now what shape they're in than during someone else's due diligence.]*

**The four I most want answered, and they're all quick:**

1. **Are our financials prepared on a cash or an accrual basis?**
2. **What do we actually produce each year — compiled statements, reviewed, audited?**
   And what would it take to move up a level if we needed to?
3. **How is carrier cost booked?** Specifically: when we pay an airline for a shipment,
   does that land in cost of goods sold, or somewhere else? And is it ever netted against
   revenue rather than shown as its own line? I'm asking because I've been calculating
   gross margin from our rate tables and I want to know whether my numbers can be
   reconciled to yours or whether I'm measuring something different.
4. **How many legal entities are there, and what sits in each?**

**Then, if there's time — things I'd like copies of:**

- **Monthly profit and loss, FY2024 through FY2026 to date.** Monthly rather than annual,
  because our business has a strong seasonal shape — January runs about 15% above average
  and June about 8% below — and annual figures hide it.
- **Balance sheets for the same period**, and the **accounts receivable aging** as it
  stands now. We pay carriers and then collect from funeral homes, so I suspect we're
  financing a meaningful amount of float and I've never actually measured it.
- **Tax returns for the last three years.**
- **The chart of accounts**, so I can see how things are categorized before I ask you
  about any of them.

**And one broader question.** If a third party — a lender, a buyer, an investor — looked
at our financials next year, **what would you expect them to take issue with, or want
normalized?** I'm not asking for figures on anything personal, and I'm not asking you to
do the work. I just want to know in advance where the soft spots are, because I'd rather
hear it from you than from someone across a table.

Happy to work through whatever's easiest on your side. If some of this is a report you can
run in five minutes and some is a project, tell me which is which and I'll prioritize.

Thanks Arty.

Ethan
