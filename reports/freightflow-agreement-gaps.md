# FreightFlow Agreement — Gaps CSI Should Close With Counsel

**NOT LEGAL ADVICE — PREPARED FOR COUNSEL REVIEW**

*Prepared by legal-risk, 2026-11-10. Periodic review of a critical vendor agreement. This is a plain-language reading of what the document says and does not say, an issue list, and questions for counsel. It does not interpret law and drafts no contract language. Analysis only: no one outside the repo has been contacted, and nothing here should leave the repo before counsel has seen it.*

**Document:** `data/raw/freightflow-agreement.pdf` (Software Development and Support Agreement, FreightFlow Software LLC and Cargo Sales Intl Inc, 46 pages). **Readability: clean.** The PDF has a good text layer (extracted with `pdftotext`, 2,076 lines, no OCR needed). Two limits on the copy itself: the **Effective Date is blank** and the **signature blocks are blank**, so this is an unsigned copy. Every clause cited below was read in the PDF text this session.

**Builds on** `reports/freightflow-agreement-review.md` (2026-11-02), which covers scope, the revenue share, IP, termination and key-person risk in depth. I spot-checked its readings of §§6.1, 7.1, 8, 9, 14.2–14.6, 17.2 and Ex. C §§2.3, 11, 12 against the PDF and they hold. This report does not repeat that work. It re-cuts the agreement around one question: **if the relationship ended, soured, changed hands, or the vendor began serving CSI's competitors, what would CSI find it had not secured?** New findings not in the 11-02 review are marked **[NEW]**.

## 0. The answer: the three gaps that matter most

**Triage: counsel required before CSI relies on this agreement for exit planning, a capital raise, or an acquisition. Counsel review recommended on everything else.**

**Gap 1. CSI's data is not clearly CSI's, and the only way to get it out is a 40-day, hourly-billed transition window.** [Severity: HIGH]
- No clause says CSI owns the data entered into or generated by the OMS. The definition of "CSI Materials" (§1.4) covers only material that CSI owns **and** that "qualif[ies] for or [is] protected by" IP rights, and it excludes anything "worked on or developed by Developer." Booking records the OMS generates could be argued outside it. The "trade secrets" carve-back in §1.4 lists rates, customer lists, partnerships and alliances, but not booking history, volumes or decedent and family records.
- The **only** data-return right is §14.5(c): on termination or expiry, for "up to forty (40) days," Developer delivers "any data or materials belonging to CSI in a format reasonably requested by CSI." It is billed at $50/hour (Ex. C §12) and is "subject to the same terms … applicable to … Support Services," which brings back the Ex. B §2.3 "commercially reasonable efforts" language. There is **no standing export right during the Term**, no format or completeness standard, no timeline, and no notice period.
- **[NEW]** §14.4(b)–(c) requires Developer to **delete or return** CSI Materials and Confidential Information on termination and certify deletion. Nothing sequences that against the 40-day transition. Counsel should say whether Developer could lawfully delete before CSI has a verified copy, or be left holding data after.
- Why this bites: CSI cannot today get a working bulk export from the admin tools (`context/DATA-INVENTORY.md`; `reports/oms-export-spec.md`). A contractual right that is vague, slow, hourly-billed and exercisable only after the relationship has already broken is the only guarantee CSI has. **If the relationship soured, CSI would be negotiating for its own records.**

**Gap 2. Nothing protects decedent and family data, and Developer's liability is capped near the fees CSI has paid it.** [Severity: HIGH]
- The only data-protection obligation on Developer is §12.2: comply with "applicable data privacy and security laws." I found **no** security standard, breach-notification duty, data-location or sub-processor term, audit right, or backup/disaster-recovery duty. **[NEW]** The §12 heading says "Technology Audit," but §12.1–12.3 contain no audit right over Developer. The warranty disclaimer (§15.2) expressly disclaims that the Work Product will "be secure."
- Liability (§17.2) is capped at "the amount paid to Developer in the preceding twelve (12) months." The fixed fee is **$6,250 × 12 = $75,000 a year**, and installments start only after Initial Deliverables Acceptance (Ex. C §§2.1–2.2). **If acceptance has not occurred or occurred recently, the amount actually paid, and so the cap, could be far below $75,000. [Unknown: acceptance date is not in the repo.]** The cap has no carve-out for data breach, confidentiality, or indemnity. §17.1 also excludes lost revenue, business interruption and "impact on business" for both sides.
- The indemnity (§16.1) covers only third-party claims "directly caused by" Developer's gross negligence, willful misconduct, a "purposeful and material" breach, or IP infringement. **Ordinary negligence is not covered.** A family or funeral-home claim that traces to an ordinary software defect would sit with CSI.
- CSI is an additional insured on Developer's general liability policy only (§11.1(a)). Developer's Tech E&O and cyber policies are $1M each, with no additional-insured status for CSI.
- **Anything touching liability for a failed shipment is counsel-required** under the legal-risk charter. A vendor-side outage or defect that causes a missed or misrouted shipment is the scenario this section exposes. Between the §17.1 exclusions, the cap, and the narrow indemnity, CSI would likely bear that loss and any claim from the family or receiving home. Whether and how CSI's own customer terms and carrier tariffs pass any of it on is a separate unread question.

**Gap 3. Continuity: no escrow, no term on the license to Developer's own components, and the pieces needed to actually run the system are unaddressed.** [Severity: HIGH]
- **No source-code escrow of any kind.** The word does not appear in the agreement. The partial substitute is §4.3(b)–(c): source for the Initial Deliverables must be uploaded to a repository "under CSI's control" and documented, as a condition of delivery. That is a one-time delivery condition. Nothing requires CSI-held source to stay current with later Change Orders or Support-driven changes, nothing requires periodic verification that it builds, and I cannot tell from the repo whether it was done.
- CSI's license to Developer's **Background Technology** (§7.1) has no stated term, and §14.4(a) and §14.6 point in different directions on survival (see the 11-02 review, I2). The only identified item is a proprietary Python "Scraping Library" that, per Ex. A, feeds the quote and facility-hours engine. **[NEW]** "Software" (§1.16) is what Developer "develops or otherwise provides under this Agreement"; whether Background Technology source is in the "Source Code" CSI must receive (§2.2(b)) is unclear.
- Hosting, domain registrar, production database, GitHub organization and airline API credentials are **not mentioned** as items CSI owns or controls (§1.2(b) of Ex. B only assigns hosting *maintenance* to Developer). **[NEW]** There is no business-continuity, backup, or step-in right.
- Exit economics make this worse: CSI **keeps owing the unpaid installments (up to $225,000) even if Developer terminates for convenience or CSI terminates for breach** (Ex. C §§2.3, 11.3), with narrow exceptions. A Sale of CSI **automatically terminates** the agreement and triggers a payment (§14.3; Ex. C §7). Transition assistance is capped at 40 days.

**Confidence:** high on what the text says; medium on how it plays out, because the executed copy, Change Orders, acceptance notices and the Feb 17, 2026 assignment are not in the repo.

## 3. The four scenarios, one line each

| If the relationship … | CSI would discover … |
|---|---|
| **Ended** (either side, 60 days) | No standing export, 40 days of billable transition, uncertain license to the Scraping Library, installments still owed, and unknown control of hosting and credentials. |
| **Soured** | The only levers are a 10-Business-Day breach cure, §5.2 withholding (which can suspend Services, per the 11-02 review), and arbitration. SLA failure gives no credits and does not stop installments. |
| **Was acquired** | §13.2(d)–(e) requires CSI approval of a Developer change of control and §19.10(b) treats it as an assignment needing CSI consent, for up to 3 years. That is a real protection. But after that window, or if the buyer is unlisted, §9.3 would not bar competitor work. |
| **Began serving CSI's competitors** | Only 22 named parties are covered, only for 18 or 24 months, with enforceability uncertain. Nothing restricts reuse of know-how beyond confidentiality. |

Also relevant to CSI's own plans: a **Sale of CSI** terminates the agreement and triggers a payment (floor $2,500 per remaining complete month, per Ex. C §7). Counsel must read it before any raise or acquisition work relies on this platform (`§7.5`).

## 4. Counsel brief

*Ranked. Ask counsel to answer, in writing, in this order. Items 1–6 are the engagement; 7–12 can follow. Not a request to draft anything until Ethan decides.*

**Hand counsel:** this report, `reports/freightflow-agreement-review.md`, the PDF, and (once gathered) the executed copy, all Change Orders, acceptance notices, the Feb 17, 2026 assignment (§18.3), and any Ellison or other subcontractor agreement. **Before anything leaves CSI, check it for decedent and family data (`§7.2`); the agreement itself contains none.**

1. **Data ownership and export.** Does CSI own all data in and generated by the OMS? Is §1.4 "CSI Materials" too narrow to capture it? What export right does CSI have during the Term (Ex. A item 8)?
2. **Exit mechanics.** How do §14.4(b)–(c) deletion, §14.5(c) delivery, and Ex. B §2.3 "commercially reasonable efforts" interact? Can Developer delete before CSI has a verified copy? Is 40 days a ceiling, and is "Customer" in §14.5 a drafting slip?
3. **Continuing license and Background Technology.** Does CSI keep a license to the Scraping Library after termination or expiry (§7.1 vs §14.4(a) vs §14.6)? Does "general or industry know-how" (§1.2) let Developer reuse OMS architecture elsewhere? Is Background Technology source part of what CSI must receive (§1.16, §2.2(b))?
4. **Installments and who §11 binds.** Does CSI owe the unpaid Initial Deliverables Fee if Developer leaves or breaches (Ex. C §§2.3, 11.3)? What is the amount outstanding given the acceptance date?
5. **Liability and data.** Given a cap of "amount paid … preceding twelve months" and the §17.1 exclusions, what is CSI's real recovery for a data breach or a failure that causes a missed shipment? What does the missing "Exhibit D" reference do to the §17.2 carve-out? Is the absence of any data-security or breach-notification term a gap counsel would close, and how?
6. **Sale of CSI and financing.** Does a recapitalization, control investment or the acquisition program trigger §14.3? What is the "bona fide financing" carve-out's reach (`§7.5` applies)?
7. **Escrow.** What are the options and rough costs for escrow or continuous source deposit with release triggers? Is CSI's repository access sufficient evidence of delivery?
8. **Competitor and know-how coverage.** Is the closed list enforceable and sufficient? Which Restricted Period governs (18 vs 24 months)? Does Developer's reuse of CSI-derived know-how for others fall inside §8 or §9? Does a Developer sale to an unlisted competitor circumvent §9.3?
9. **Confidentiality.** Does §8 clearly cover rates, customer lists, volumes and personal data? What is the effect of the five-year sunset on non-trade-secret information, and the §8.4 / §14.4(c) inconsistency?
10. **Change Order practice.** Is the Ex. B §1 list exhaustive? Do Error Response hours count in the 40 (S2)? Is the practice "CSI states its read, Developer classifies" consistent with §§2.4 and 19.5, and does informal work create any rights or waiver?
11. **Insurance.** Are Developer's $1M limits and non-additional-insured Tech E&O and cyber policies adequate for decedent-data exposure? Which of CSI's own policies respond?
12. **Housekeeping.** Is there an executed version identical to this one? What is the Effective Date, and does it move the Term end? Fix the broken cross-references (the review lists them).

**Counsel required before CSI does any of these:** sends a dispute, breach, withholding or renewal notice; signs a Change Order, amendment or waiver; changes Developer's access to the repo, hosting or credentials; engages or hires Developer Personnel directly; moves OMS work to another vendor; describes the platform's ownership to an investor, lender or buyer.
