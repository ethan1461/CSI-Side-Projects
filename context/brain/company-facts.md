## What CSI can and cannot physically handle

- **CORRECTION — export-only for physical handling.** CSI physically handles shipments
  **leaving** the U.S., not imports. This applies only to physically handling human
  remains. Technology, referrals, and consular/document support apply worldwide.
- **Inbound (into-U.S.) model:** a licensed customs partner from CSI's network clears the
  remains at the arrival gateway; CSI then handles the domestic onward leg and final
  delivery anywhere in the U.S. For U.S.-bound imports CSI refers partners to its network
  (often partners at Bergen). The network is CSI's core strength.
- U.S. regulations bar CSI from issuing air waybills for shipments that do not touch the
  U.S. For non-U.S. lanes CSI can only help partners pick the right carrier.
- Any copy that says CSI itself clears customs on inbound shipments needs review against
  this before it goes out. Flag it; do not silently rewrite approved copy. Known stale
  copy is listed in `deliverables-index.md`.
- **CSI holds no TSA or IATA certification**; affiliate Southern Cross Trading does
  (`initiatives/federal-contracting.md`) [e1245953, 2026-09-29].

## Pricing and business model

CSI earns in **two ways** (confirmed by Ethan, 2026-11-01):

1. **The rate spread.** Funeral homes pay the published airline rate with nothing added
   and no booking fee; CSI's cost is the lower contracted rate. Measured gross margin:
   ~35% domestic, ~41% international on contracted lanes. Quote only with the limits in
   `reports/carrier-margin-resolved.md` (rate-card margin, unweighted by volume,
   excludes cost to serve, no cost data for most international carriers). The Feb 2026
   "30–60% discount" figure is stale.
2. **Fees for secondary services** — consulate support, pickup and drop-off, and other
   ancillary charges. **Margins on these are unknown.** Requested from the data owner
   (`data-owners.md`, request #1). Until it arrives, no agent estimates them.
