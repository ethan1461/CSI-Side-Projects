Cargo Sales International (CSI) – FY26 Growth Plan

Draft V0 | Prepared: January 05, 2026

# 1. Executive Summary

FY26 objective: scale CSI’s revenue and profitability by accelerating market share capture in death care, unlocking distribution through software and adjacent verticals, and creating operating leverage via automation.

FY25 closed at ~$14.5M revenue (management reporting) with a roughly 50/50 split between international and domestic shipments. FY26 target is ≥$15.0M with an emphasis on higher-quality, scalable revenue (enterprise groups, embedded distribution) and improved contribution margin.

The plan is built around three reinforcing pillars:

- Pillar A – Core death care share capture: penetrate major funeral home groups and high-volume independents through disciplined outbound + trade show conversion.

- Pillar B – Distribution-led expansion: embed CSI within death care software platforms (revenue share) and pursue government contracting opportunities that expand addressable air cargo beyond human remains.

- Pillar C – Automation and cost leverage: deploy the new internal system to reduce manual work, redeploy 1–2 ops agent FTE equivalent into sales capacity, and improve speed/consistency of service.

# 2. Baseline: CSI Today

CSI is a specialized logistics coordinator for human remains, acting as the operational middleman between funeral homes and air carriers. CSI differentiates through fast booking turnaround, broad route coverage (domestic and international), rate competitiveness, 24/7 access to live team members, and end-to-end handling of complex documentation (particularly on international repatriations).

Scale and mix:

- Revenue: FY25 ~$14.5M (management reporting). FY24 internal pull totals ~$14.2M.

- Mix: ~50% international / 50% domestic.

- Team: 6–8 ops agents supporting execution; 2–3 sales reps driving growth.

Growth constraint today: CSI has grown incrementally over the last five years, but growth has been limited by (i) fragmented, relationship-driven buying behavior in funeral homes, (ii) change resistance within larger groups already partnered with incumbents, and (iii) operational bandwidth required to deliver white-glove service at scale. FY26 addresses these constraints with a more aggressive, systematized sales motion and automation-driven operating leverage.

# 3. FY26 Goals and Success Criteria

Top-line (growth):

- Hit ≥$15.0M revenue (floor) while building a repeatable engine for step-function growth in FY27.

- Win meaningful penetration in major funeral home groups (SCI/Dignity, Carriage, Foundation Partners, Park Lawn) through targeted account plans and field presence.

- Launch at least 1 software distribution partnership to drive incremental bookings with low CAC.

- Submit bids for a defined set of government contract opportunities and secure at least 1 pilot award or subcontract that expands CSI’s operational scope.

Bottom-line (efficiency):

- Roll out the new internal system and realize measurable reductions in manual handling time per booking.

- Achieve 1–2 FTE equivalent ops capacity release (via automation) and reinvest into sales/BD capacity.

- Improve contribution margin through reduced rework, faster document turnaround, and tighter process controls.

# 4. Pillar A: Core Death Care Share Capture

Strategy: take share from incumbents by focusing on high-volume accounts where CSI’s value proposition is most differentiated (speed, international complexity, coverage, service). The primary focus is enterprise funeral home groups and high-volume regionals/independents.

A.1 Target Segments and ICP

- Enterprise groups (priority): SCI/Dignity Memorial, Carriage Services, Foundation Partners, Park Lawn.

- Regional chains (20–150 locations): high-volume operators with centralized purchasing but local autonomy. Think Heritage Family, Ortiz, etc.

- High-volume independents: particularly those with frequent international shipments.

A.2 Sales Motion (Daily Discipline) – Ethan

CSI will implement a structured outbound cadence to ensure consistent volume and measurable conversion:

- 5–10 targeted outreach calls/week focused on Tier 1–2 accounts.

- Same-day follow-up email after any meaningful call connection.

- Weekly review of pipeline by tier: Not Contacted → Contacted → Meeting Held → Trial Shipment → Repeat Shipments.

- Account-based plays for large groups: map decision-makers (corporate + regional + location-level) and tailor messaging by persona.

A.3 Offer and Messaging

Messaging pillars:

- Price and optionality: multiple routing options, no unnecessary fees, transparent quotes.

- Speed and reliability: rapid booking turnaround, 24/7 live support, proactive issue resolution.

- International superiority: route coverage and documentation expertise (translations, apostilles/consular paperwork where required).

- Ease of switching: low-friction trial shipment; CSI handles the heavy lift (paperwork and coordination).

# 6. Pillar B: Distribution-Led Expansion

## 6.1 Software Platform Partnerships

Objective: achieve distribution and low-CAC growth by embedding CSI into death care software workflows. Target partners include Afterword, Funeral365, FuneralOne, and OneDirector.

Proposed commercial model:

- CSI is featured as the preferred shipping/logistics option inside the partner platform.

- Partner receives a revenue share per shipment booked through the platform.

- CSI provides an API or integration layer (as feasible) plus standardized SLA and pricing logic.

- Joint go-to-market: co-marketing campaigns to partner’s funeral home base and shared lead tracking.

## 6.2 Government Contracting (Adjacent Vertical)

Objective: expand CSI’s addressable market by bidding on government logistics work that can leverage CSI’s airline network beyond human remains. SAM.gov registration is complete; FY26 will focus on building a repeatable bid pipeline.

Initial focus areas:

- Federal and state agencies with recurring transport/logistics needs.

- Disaster response-related logistics and time-sensitive transport (where compliant and feasible).

- Subcontracting pathways with prime contractors to reduce early-stage burden and accelerate awards.

# 7. Pillar C: Automation, Operating Leverage, and Reinvestment

CSI is investing in a new internal system to automate booking creation, documentation workflows, routing/quote processes, and operational handoffs.

Expected outcomes:

- Reduced time-to-book and fewer manual errors, improving customer experience and repeat rate.

- Ops capacity release (1–2 FTE equivalent over time) without compromising service levels.

- Reinvestment of released capacity into sales/BD headcount and/or expanded marketing execution.

- Improved reporting and analytics to support a PE-grade narrative and internal decision-making.

# 8. KPI Framework and Operating Cadence

CSI will manage FY26 with a small set of leading and lagging indicators. Metrics are designed to be investor-grade while remaining operationally actionable.

Growth KPIs (lagging):

- Revenue (monthly, YTD) and revenue growth.

- Bookings volume and revenue per booking.

- Mix: domestic vs international; enterprise vs independent.

Sales KPIs (leading):

- Outbound activity: calls/day, connects, meetings set, meetings held.

- Conversion funnel: Not Contacted → Contacted → Meeting → Trial Shipment → Repeat.

- New logos won; reactivated logos; net revenue retention (when definable).

Efficiency KPIs:

- Ops handle time per booking (pre/post system).

- Error/rework rate; documentation cycle time for international.

- Contribution margin per booking (where measurable).

Operating cadence:

- Weekly: sales pipeline review, outbound performance, trade-show follow-ups, and top blockers.

- Monthly: KPI dashboard review (growth + efficiency) and pricing/routing learnings.

- Quarterly: roadmap progress (system rollout), headcount plan, and partnership status.

# 9. Key Risks and Mitigations

- Enterprise change resistance: Use low-friction trial shipments, highlight international advantage, and build multi-threaded stakeholder maps.

- Sales capacity limits: Codify outbound system and templates; reinvest automation savings into sales; leverage partner distribution.

- System rollout delays: Stage rollout by workflow; track adoption; maintain rollback processes.

- Government contracting complexity: Start with subcontracting and narrowly scoped bids; build a repeatable bid calendar and compliance checklist.
