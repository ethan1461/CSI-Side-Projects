## 1. Verdict

**No. The category cannot support a 17x platform multiple, and the thesis as framed does not survive. A smaller version of it might.**

- **The category is about $75M a year** `[ESTIMATE]`, bounded **$35M–$135M**, on roughly 55,000 shipments (range 30,000–90,000). Your $75–150M arithmetic holds up. My centre sits at the bottom of your range, and I would not pitch above $135M.
- **CSI already holds about 20% of it** (range 11%–43%). A roll-up here moves share between owners. It does not enter new market.
- **The profit pool is the binding number.** `[ESTIMATE]` At CSI's 19.2% EBITDA margin the whole category earns ~$14M a year (range $7M–$26M). CSI's ~$2.9M is about 20% of that. Getting to the ~$4.9M that justifies $49M at 10x means owning **~34% of every dollar of profit in the category** at central, ~73% at the low end, ~19% at the high end.
- **The AI labour argument has almost nothing to work on.** The category's gross profit pool is ~$26–30M at central. All labour in the category is paid from that, so the automatable labour pool is a fraction of it. US accounting services are $220bn, about 3,000 times larger. GC's $6T is about 80,000 times larger, nearly five orders of magnitude, not three to four.
- **Direction fails the published growth screen.** Burial is falling about 4.3% a year in NFDA's projection. Applied to CSI's volume, that is somewhere between 0% and −3.5% a year, and CSI has run flat in practice (+1.6% bookings 2023→2025). Revenue has grown 1.8% a year, mostly yield.
- **What a category this size can support** is a dominant niche operator at **8–10x EBITDA**. SCI is reported buying independents at 8–10x. Asset-light logistics trades at higher multiples only at scale. That is a good outcome. It is not 17x, and it comes from share and margin, not from AI.

**Confidence: medium that this is the right answer, low on every number inside it.** The verdict holds across my whole range, including the high case (see §6). It would change if (a) a public or partner source showed the category is several times larger than $135M, most plausibly a large airline-direct domestic flow I cannot see, or (b) a strategic buyer, not a financial one, wanted CSI's customer access. Neither is in evidence.

**What follows from the verdict.** Run the operating playbook (capacity as growth, margin as lever) and pursue the 8–10x outcome through margin and bolt-ons. Drop the platform-multiple narrative from the equity story unless a strategic-buyer thesis is built separately.

## 2. The size of the category

**Definition.** US-origin human remains moved by air: domestic (including territories) and international outbound, both brokered and booked directly with airlines. Excludes inbound (CSI does not physically clear inbound, `CLAUDE.md` §1) and the funeral home's own services (embalming, casket, ground).

**There is no published count of US remains shipments.** I searched mortality, funeral, airline and consular sources. The only industry-wide figure is IATA's "over 800,000 human and animal remain shipments" a year, which is global and mostly animals, so it is unusable. Everything below is therefore built, not read.

## 4. Direction of travel

**This is the screen CSI fails, so it is sized.**

| Measure | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| US cremation rate, 2024 | 61.8–61.9% | CANA, NFDA release `[VERIFIED — secondary]` |
| 2025 (projected) | 63.4% cremation, 31.6% burial | [NFDA 2025 Cremation & Burial Report](https://nfda.org/Portals/0/10-06-2025--2025%20C%26B%20Report%20Trade%20Release.pdf) `[VERIFIED]` |
| 2045 (projected) | 82.3% cremation, 13.0% burial | same |
| Burial decline | **−4.3%/yr compound** 2025→2045 (−4.8% in 2024→25) | `[ESTIMATE]` from the two rows above |
| Deaths | +0.7% 2024→25 | NCHS, above |

Deaths are rising slightly with the ageing population (CBO has deaths exceeding births from the early 2030s). That partly offsets burial decline. It does not reverse it.

**What it does to shipments.** Whole-body air transport needs a casketed or prepared body. Cremated remains travel as baggage or by mail, which is a large share of what funeral homes do not need a broker for. How tightly shipped volume follows burial share is the unknown. Three cases, deaths +0.8%/yr:

| Sensitivity to burial share | Annual volume change | 3-year effect |
|---|---|---|
| Fully proportional (1.0) | −3.5% | **−10%** |
| Half (0.5) | −1.4% | −4% |
| Weak (0.3) | −0.5% | −1.5% |

**What the evidence says.** CSI bookings grew +1.6% from 2023 to 2025 (10,840 → 11,013, geography workbook) while burial share fell. So for CSI's mix, sensitivity is **0.3 or lower, possibly negative** because diaspora growth offsets. I would plan on a 0.3–0.5 range: **−0.5% to −1.4% a year in volume**.

Two things cut against comfort:

1. **The diaspora buffer is not immune.** Latino Californians moved from burial-majority to cremation-majority in 2020 ([Sacramento Bee analysis, via inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/cremations-surpassed-burials-among-california-latinos-during-the-pandemic-here-s-why)). The buffer is eroding in the community that supplies much of CSI's volume. Single source, one state.
2. **The foreign-born population is falling.** CPS shows 53.3M (Jan 2025) to 51.9M (Jun 2025). The ACS shows slight growth to mid-2025 (50.4M). The surveys disagree and I do not know which is right. If the CPS is right, it is a second headwind on corridor demand, arriving with a lag.

**Against the published screen** (market growing faster than GDP): category revenue growth is volume (−1.4% to +1%) plus yield (+1% to +3%), roughly **−0.5% to +4%**. CSI's own 2023–25 revenue CAGR was **1.8%**. This is below any reasonable nominal GDP growth rate. I did not fetch a GDP series, so verify before quoting. **CSI fails the criterion, and the failure is structural.**

**State-level cremation rates** were not obtainable (NFDA's state tables are behind a paywall, $175). I cannot yet tell you where erosion bites first. Highest-cremation states are in the West. CSI's volume is concentrated in FL (31%), NY (19%), NJ (9%), MD (7%), per the geography workbook. Buying the NFDA report is the cheapest next step.

## 6. Does the verdict survive the high case?

Yes. The test is simply what share of the profit pool CSI would need, at each multiple, to reach ~$49M of EV.

| Category | Pool EBITDA | CSI today | Needed at 10x ($4.9M) | at 8x ($6.1M) | at 6x ($8.2M) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low, $35M | $6.7M | 43% | 73% | 91% | >100% |
| **Central, $74M** | **$14.2M** | **20%** | **34%** | **43%** | **58%** |
| High, $135M | $25.9M | 11% | 19% | 24% | 32% |

`[ESTIMATE]` Pool = category revenue × CSI's 19.2% YTD EBITDA margin (`the-25m-gap.md` §1). That margin is CSI's and `[ASSUMPTION]` for everyone else, and the pool counts only brokered volume's margin, so it is generous. Needed share = EV ÷ multiple ÷ pool.

- **At 17x on today's EBITDA** the $49M target needs no share gain. It needs a buyer to pay a software-like multiple for a freight broker. Freight-broker comps run **3.0–7.5x** in the lower middle market ([CT Acquisitions](https://www.ctacquisitions.com/freight-broker-business-for-sale/), a marketing source) and **4–8x** ([Sofer Advisors, June 2026](https://soferadvisors.com/insights/blog/how-to-value-a-logistics-or-3pl-company-in-2026/)). Asset-light logistics trades at ~13x on a PitchBook/Axial figure that reflects large-cap companies, not a $3M-EBITDA niche. **I found no precedent for a ~$15M-revenue niche broker priced at 17x.**
- **At 10x on ~$4.9M** it needs ~$2M of acquired EBITDA. At central that is 14% of the pool (~$11M outside CSI) held by competitors who may not want to sell. It is feasible at central and high, **not at low**.
- **The remaining route is a strategic buyer.** SCI trades at ~12x EV/EBITDA (sources differ, 11.9–12.7x, undated) and reportedly buys independents at 8–10x. That is the only path with precedent above 10x, and it values CSI's funeral-home access, not its category size. This team does not approach anyone (§7.5). I am noting it as the one thesis that could differ from my verdict.

**Antitrust note, not a conclusion.** At 34% share of a niche in which the other owners are also customers and allies, structure matters. This is for counsel, not for me.
