## The six judgements

1. **Integration consent is negotiable and is not the Developer's core economics.** A migration
   or integration Change Order is paid work for the Developer (§2.4 expects Change Orders to
   carry remuneration; the support rate is $50/hour). A pre-agreed scope definition and rate card
   for migrating an acquired book gives the Developer certainty and gives CSI a known cost. **Your
   Route 1 "pre-agreed migration scope" item is the right headline and I rate it the most
   achievable high-value ask.** The real constraint is not willingness but **capacity**: a small
   vendor with a 40-hour monthly support bucket cannot promise turnaround for several deals a
   year. So the ask has two halves, a price and a capacity or turnaround commitment, and the
   second is the harder one.

2. **There is a question that may weaken the "a third party can decline each integration"
   premise, and it needs counsel before anyone repeats it.** The Change Order requirement
   governs work the *Developer* does. Whether CSI, which owns the Work Product under §6.1(a), may
   have its own staff or a different developer do an integration on that code is not answered in
   either of my reports. It turns on the Background Technology licence (I2), confidentiality (§8),
   and §9.1 if the new developer is drawn from Developer Personnel. If the answer is yes, CSI's
   dependence is practical, not legal, and **Route 3b gets materially stronger** (CSI-owned
   capacity beside the Developer, no displacement). If no, the premise holds. I am adding this as
   a new counsel item (N1).

3. **The revenue share rate schedule is core economics to the Developer. Do not treat Route 4 as a
   cheap early fix.** The cash is modest (your arithmetic stands) but it is the Developer's
   return on the build and it scales with success. Converting the schedule to a fixed or capped
   amount is a real trade and should be bundled with something the Developer values (a Term
   extension is the obvious candidate). **Within Route 4, three sub-items are more tradeable than
   the rate itself:** how acquired-book revenue is treated (CSI buys that revenue at a multiple and,
   if those customers order through the portal, the Developer shares in it), audit mutuality
   (§5.4), and the 30% subscription share on the SaaS path, which is the term that bears on the
   multiple.

4. **§9.1 (no hiring Kyri, Alex or Developer Personnel) is not a lever to build a route on.** It is
   mutual, it protects the Developer's people, and a free-hire right is the item I expect most
   resistance on. Any route that assumes CSI employs the builders should be treated as low
   probability. The achievable version is a defined mechanism (conversion fee, or a right that
   arises on a stated event such as the Developer ceasing to operate), plus documented
   knowledge transfer and a named qualified backup. Employment-law questions are counsel's (§7.7).
   Also: Kyri is Developer Personnel and must not be described as CSI's CTO in anything outside
   CSI.

5. **Route 0 is not a stable endpoint, because the agreement ends.** The Term runs to about
   1 March 2029 (subject to T1, the blank Effective Date) with no automatic renewal. A programme
   that starts buying books in 2027 meets expiry mid-programme, and expiry triggers 40 days of
   billable transition. So **a renewal or successor conversation happens regardless of strategy.**
   The question is only whether CSI opens it, on its own timetable and with the hygiene items on
   the table, or has it opened for it. Dates: Developer's binding renewal notice about 1 Dec 2028,
   CSI's about 30 Jan 2029. That strengthens Route 1 and Route 4 as a package and weakens Route 0
   as a "do nothing" comparison.

6. **Route 2 (acquire the Developer's interest) prices the Developer's core economics, not its
   hygiene terms.** Treat the revenue share and the subscription share as the purchase price drivers
   (your single-fact note about Donald's reconciliation is correct). Route 2 also does not solve
   N1 or §9.1 unless the people come with it. Your observation that CSI holds the §13.2(d)-(e)
   consent right on a Developer sale stands and I would add it to the leverage list in the
   amendment scope.
