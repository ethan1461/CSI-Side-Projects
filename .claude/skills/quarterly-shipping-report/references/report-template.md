# Report Template

This is the fixed spine of the CSI Quarterly Shipping Report. Keep these sections, in this order, every quarter — the consistency is what turns the report into a recognizable franchise. The *Trend Spotlight* topic rotates; everything else recurs.

Target length: 4–7 pages / roughly 1,200–2,000 words. Long enough to be authoritative, short enough that a director finishes it.

Title block, every issue:
> **The CSI Human Remains Shipping Report**
> *Q[X] [YYYY] · Cargo Sales International*
> A quarterly read on how the world moves loved ones home.

---

## 1. The Quarter at a Glance

Three to five bolded headline takeaways, one sentence each. This is what a busy reader sees and forwards. Each headline should be a *finding*, not a topic.

- Bad (topic): "International corridors."
- Good (finding): "Mexico and Central America remained CSI's busiest international corridor, with demand strongest ahead of the holidays."

Pull the headlines from the sections below after drafting them, so they're real.

## 2. By the Numbers — Volume & Velocity

The quarter's movement, told plainly and respectfully. Use only real figures; otherwise go directional or `[FILL: …]`.

Cover where data allows:
- Total loved ones moved this quarter, and the direction vs. last quarter / same quarter last year.
- The split across CSI's eight gateways (JFK, IAD, MIA, BOS, DFW, IAH, LAX, SFO) — which carried the most, and any shift.
- Speed/velocity if available — average time-to-confirmed-booking is a strong, on-brand stat because it's CSI's differentiator (no 3-day interline waits). Frame as service insight, not a brag.

A single clean chart works well here (volume trend or gateway split). See `data-and-research.md`.

## 3. Where Loved Ones Traveled — Top International Corridors

The heart of the report. Rank the top international destinations/corridors this quarter and note what moved. CSI's known high-volume corridors: Mexico & Latin America, Israel, China, the Philippines & Pacific/Asia, and Europe.

For each notable corridor: where, the direction of demand, and *one* human or operational note (a holiday, a season, a consular quirk). This is where the report feels expert — it shows CSI sees patterns a single funeral home can't.

If exact rankings aren't in the data, rank only what's supported and mark the rest `[FILL: …]`.

## 4. The CSI Lane Watch — World Events Affecting Shipping

The recurring, research-driven section readers will come for. What happened this quarter in the world that changes how remains move? Each item: the event, and — critically — what it means for a corridor CSI serves.

Source these from `web_search` (see the checklist in `data-and-research.md`); cite them. Typical material: airline route or capacity changes, airport/customs disruptions, conflicts or instability along a corridor, consular/documentation changes, weather, and predictable peak periods. 2–4 items, each a short paragraph.

## 5. Trend Spotlight (rotating)

One deeper theme per issue — the "thought leadership" centerpiece. Rotate so the report stays fresh. Candidate themes, several of which map directly to CSI's strategic challenges:
- Cremation vs. full-body repatriation and what shifting end-of-life trends mean for long-distance transport.
- The rise of Latin America repatriation and the documentation/consular load it carries.
- Peak-season planning: when corridors get tight and how far ahead to book.
- Consular and known-shipper bottlenecks, and how lead times are really set.
- Why routing flexibility (multiple gateways) changes options and pricing for a family.

~300–500 words. This is the section most likely to be excerpted into a standalone LinkedIn thought piece.

## 6. What This Means for Funeral Homes

The payoff. Translate everything above into 3–5 concrete, actionable takeaways a director can use: a lead time to plan for, a question to ask a transport partner, a document to have ready, a corridor to expect pressure on. Practical and generous — no strings. This section is why the report gets forwarded.

## 7. Looking Ahead

A short, forward-looking close: 2–3 things to watch next quarter (an upcoming peak, a corridor to monitor, a known schedule change). Leaves the reader anticipating the next issue.

## 8. About CSI (standing footer)

A tight, credible 2–3 sentence block — what CSI is and the byline. Vendor-neutral in posture, factual in substance: 23 years moving loved ones worldwide, agents at MIA, JFK, IAD, IAH, and LAX, no booking fees, a dedicated specialist on call 24/7/365. Include a single contact line / CTA. Keep it understated — the report already did the selling by being good.

> *Methodology note (optional):* one line on the data source ("Figures reflect shipments handled by CSI during Q[X] [YYYY]") so the numbers read as grounded, not promotional.
