## 1. Why gateway handling specifically

**What it is.** The physical and documentary work at the gateway: receiving the shipment,
checking and assembling documentation, preparation for carriage, known-shipper compliance,
the airline handover, and on the inbound side customs clearance and onward dispatch.

**Why it is the strongest lane on the evidence:**

- **CSI already buys this capability in rather than owning it.** CSI's physical handling is
  **export-only**; for inbound, a licensed customs partner clears at the arrival gateway and
  CSI runs the domestic onward leg (`CLAUDE.md` §1). The capability gap is structural and
  permanent, not a staffing shortfall.
- **It converts a cost line into a margin line.** CSI's margin today is the spread between
  the published rate and the contracted carrier rate, plus secondary service fees
  (`CLAUDE.md` §1). Gateway handling sits outside both. Margin is the lever that matters
  under an enterprise-value objective, because it compounds twice — it raises earnings and
  it raises what a buyer pays for them.
- **It is the one lane where the automation argument actually pays.** The work is
  rule-bound, document-heavy, repetitive and failure-intolerant, and it is substantially
  the *same* work whatever the corridor. That matters because under the roll-up criteria
  **overlap beats complement**: more of the same rule-bound work on a system that already
  encodes its rules, rather than a new corridor needing a new rulebook.
- **It improves the product, not just the P&L.** Faster handover, fewer airline rejections,
  and the delivery confirmation CSI cannot offer at the European end today — which is
  exactly what the Rowland conversation is about.

**Honest caveat:** nobody has sized this lane. `gtm-strategist` has been tasked with it.
**Do not put a number on it in this conversation.**

## 3. For Cheryl to carry: five questions

Open-ended, in order. The first four are about Bergen's operation; the fifth is a gift.

1. **How does the gateway and documentation side actually run today?** Volumes, locations,
   how the team is organised, which parts are still manual.
2. **Where does it break?** What is the failure mode that costs Bergen the most — a missed
   flight, a rejected document, a customs hold, something else.
3. **What would it take to handle meaningfully more volume with the people you have?** Not
   "would you like more volume" — the constraint is the interesting part.
4. **Is there appetite to do more of this together, and what shape would Bergen want it to
   take?** Let Matt describe the shape. Do not propose one.
5. **What do you wish CSI did differently at handover?** Ask it last, mean it, and write
   the answer down.

## 4. The line that must not be crossed, and a sentence for when it drifts

**Bergen is a partner, a major customer, and a company that also appears on competitor
lists.** That combination is exactly where antitrust exposure lives, and a conversation
about jointly handling shipments can drift into it without anyone intending to.

**Safe ground — capability and capacity:** how the work gets done, what breaks, what it
would take to do more of it, what a joint capability might look like as a service offered
on its own terms.

**Off limits, in both directions, and not negotiable (`§7.5`):**

- What either company charges its customers. No rates, no fees, no discussing CSI's
  published rate or Bergen's pricing.
- Which customers, homes, territories or corridors "belong" to whom. **Market division is
  the one that would look worst in hindsight and is the easiest to wander into** when two
  companies with overlapping footprints discuss working together.
- Coordinating on any bid or tender.
- Anything about CSI's acquisition thinking, any target, or any valuation.

**If the conversation drifts, a sentence that closes it without damaging anything:**

> *"Let's keep this on how the work gets done — I'd rather our lawyers set the frame before
> we get anywhere near commercial terms."*

That is true, it is friendly, and it is the right answer.

  handling operation needs most and can least easily generate.
- **Eight gateways** — JFK, IAD, MIA, BOS, DFW, IAH, LAX, SFO, with ORD in progress — and
  pickup service in MIA, IAH and LAX.
