## The note

**Subject:** What the shipment history showed, and how we resource the follow-up

Cheryl, Donald,

Donald — thank you for the sales-by-customer file, and for pushing me to get the shipment history.
Both landed, and together they answered a question we have never been able to answer.

**What the data showed.**

Donald, your read was mostly right and one part needs correcting.

Confirmed: Bergen is growing, the Ortiz homes are declining — further than you put it, down about
35% from their 2023 peak — and we do lose some and win some every year, consistently, going back to
2017. The SCI locations are up. And the FPG cohort from two years ago was real: 22 locations and 246
bookings in 2024, which was over half that year's entire net growth, completely hidden inside a flat
year. **Grouping the Bergen and Ortiz entities the way you specified was essential** — read
separately, two of the Rivera homes look like a collapse beside a triumph. Grouped, it is one group
declining.

**The correction is on the conclusion.** We assumed, and you put it plainly, that the way to the
next level is adding a lot more funeral homes. That is necessary but it is not sufficient, and the
reason is in the numbers:

- We are winning more accounts than ever — the active base is up **32.7% in two years**, 411 new
  funeral homes in the last twelve months
- But of the homes that shipped with us in 2023, **349 have placed no booking at all this year**,
  taking about **1,067 bookings a year** with them
- Three years of acquisition added 1,976 bookings. **Churn consumed 54% of that**
- Net result over three years: **+692 bookings**

So the engine works. It leaks. At current first-year survival, doubling what we win roughly doubles
the leak alongside the gain.

**One number to be careful with.** Churn is 35% by account count but only **5.6% weighted by
volume** — we keep our real customers and lose small, infrequent ones in bulk. Accounts shipping
four or more times a year churn at 9.5%. I would not want 35% quoted anywhere outside this room; it
describes a company we are not.

**What we are doing about it.**

We have a ranked list of about 400 named funeral homes, each with its own booking history and a
reason to call. Otto and Stacy are taking theirs this week — 101 and 108 accounts each.

**Two things I need from you both.**

**1. The biggest accounts need an owner, and I think it should be Cheryl.**

Any home shipping 50 or more a year has come off the rep lists entirely. That is nine accounts —
including Del Pueblo, Neshama and the Ortiz homes — and between them they carry **680 bookings a
year, which is 52% of everything at risk.**

They are relationships rather than accounts, and a clumsy call costs more than the call could
possibly win. **Cheryl, would you take these, or tell me who should?** Vadim and I can work them
with you, but I do not want them sitting unowned while the smaller ones get called.

**2. The follow-up work does not pay anyone, and that will show.**

None of this counts as new business, so none of it touches Otto's or Stacy's commission. We would be
asking them to spend weeks on work that earns them nothing, and that tends to resolve itself the
wrong way.

Two ideas, and the decision is yours:

- **Treat any home that has not shipped with us in two years as new business**, regardless of
  territory. From the rep's side that is close to what it is — starting a relationship from nothing
- **Open a portion of the list more widely in the office**, with a bump in bonus if a home someone
  calls ships with us before year end. It would take load off Otto and Stacy and give people a way
  to go above and beyond

I have no view on which, and I am not going to pretend to price either — that is yours to work out.
What I am confident about is that **if retention work does not pay, it will not happen consistently
no matter who we assign it to**, and that is a bigger question than this one campaign.

The second idea also depends on whether the booking desk can spare the hours. **I am asking Vadim
separately** — if he says it adds pressure we cannot afford, we will find another way.

Happy to walk either of you through the full analysis whenever suits.

Ethan
