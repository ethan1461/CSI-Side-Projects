# The $25M gap — what the growth programme actually has to deliver

**Date:** 2026-11-04 · **Sources:** Donald (CFO), accrual-basis figures relayed by Ethan 2026-11-04; Ethan on the ownership objective, 2026-11-03
**Status:** **Internal planning only.** Nothing here goes to any outsider. No valuation is asserted — every multiple below is a published-market reference point, labelled. `§7.5` applies in full.
**Confidence:** High on CSI's own figures. **Medium-low on the multiple range**, which is the number that matters most and which only a banker or counsel can properly set.

---

## The answer

**Volume growth cannot close this gap. The arithmetic is not close.**

The +5% bookings target set on 2026-11-03 is worth roughly **$220K of EBITDA**. The gap between CSI today and the CEO's $25M number is somewhere between **$2M and $4M of EBITDA.**

That is a factor of ten. **Margin work and acquisitions are the only levers with the right order of magnitude** — which means the "volume wins" ruling, made before the $25M objective was stated, should be revisited.

## 1. What CSI actually earns

Two things worth noting immediately.

**The gross margin cross-checks.** $12.5M − $8.2M = **$4.3M, or 34.4%.** That sits almost exactly on the ~35% domestic rate-card figure in `reports/carrier-margin-resolved.md`, derived independently from the rate tables. **Two unrelated methods agreeing is the strongest validation either has had.**

**EBITDA margin is 19.2% year to date.** `[ESTIMATE]` Carried to ~$15M full-year revenue at the same rate: **~$2.9M EBITDA for FY26.**

**One caveat that cuts the other way, and a buyer would seize on it.** Donald describes deliberately suppressing reported year-end profit — *"We stop cashing checks in mid-Nov and pay off all our bills to keep our profit down."* That is ordinary tax management, but it means **reported profit understates earnings power**, and the add-back is a normalisation question for Arty and counsel, not something to estimate here. Cash-basis profit today is $3.03M against $1.7M for all of last year, which is the same effect visible from the other side.

## 2. What $25M implies

Ethan, 2026-11-03: to sell a majority stake (51%+), the CEO would need to receive close to **$25M**.

| | |
|---|---|
| Implied whole-enterprise value | **~$49M** |
| Against FY26 revenue (~$15M) | **~3.3x revenue** |
| Against FY26 EBITDA (~$2.9M) | **~17x EBITDA** |

**Logistics brokers are valued on EBITDA, not revenue**, and published lower-middle-market multiples in freight brokerage and forwarding generally sit in the **4x–8x** range. A niche, long-established, high-gross-margin operator can command the upper end or beyond; CSI's concentration, key-person exposure and lack of audited financials pull the other way.

**`[ESTIMATE]` — what EBITDA $49M would require:**

| Multiple | EBITDA needed | Versus ~$2.9M today |
|---|---|---|
| 6x | **$8.2M** | 2.8x |
| 8x | **$6.1M** | 2.1x |
| 10x (generous for the niche) | **$4.9M** | 1.7x |

**Even assuming normalisation adds $0.5–1M**, the requirement lands around **$4M–$7M of EBITDA**. The gap is **$2M to $4M**.

**This is not a reason to abandon the number.** It is the reason to know it now rather than in a process. One of three things is true, and they lead to different years:

1. The multiple is achievable at the top of the range with the right story → the gap is nearer $2M and the programme is about margin quality and diversification.
2. The number needs real EBITDA growth → the gap is $3–4M and **acquisitions are required, not optional.**
3. The number is not reachable by 2027 → **that is the most valuable thing anyone could tell the principal**, and it should be said plainly rather than discovered.

## 3. Sizing the levers against the gap

| Lever | `[ESTIMATE]` annual EBITDA | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **+5% bookings** (the FY27 target) | **~$220K** | ~550 incremental bookings × ~$400 contribution |
| **Carrier selection** | **$20K–$400K** | `reports/carrier-selection-lane-evidence.md`; $205.64 average gap, frequency unknown |
| **The $500 international margin floor** | **$550K–$1.1M** | ~5,500 international shipments; depends entirely on the current average, which nobody has measured |
| **Secondary services** | **unknown** | Charged "at cost for the most part" (Ethan). Pure upside, unquantified |
| **Acquisitions** | **the balance** | Inorganic EBITDA, bought rather than grown |

**Add every organic lever at its optimistic end and it comes to roughly $1.7M.** That closes between a half and a third of the gap, and only if everything lands.

**So acquisitions are not an ambition sitting alongside the growth plan. They are the arithmetic requirement.** Ethan's instinct — *"All I know is we want to acquire and grow"* — is the correct conclusion, and this is the reasoning that supports it.

## 4. What this says about the FY27 ruling

On 2026-11-03 Ethan ruled **"volume wins"** when volume and margin compete for scarce resource. He stated the $25M objective **afterwards**.

**With the number in view, the ranking inverts.** The $500 international margin floor alone is worth **two to five times** what the entire +5% volume target is worth, and it needs no new customers, no acquisition cost and no sales capacity — the constraint everywhere else.

Two further points:

- **Margin also moves the multiple, not just the numerator.** A buyer pays more for $4M of EBITDA on diversified, well-priced revenue than for $4M scraped from volume at thin margin. Under an enterprise-value objective, margin quality compounds twice.
- **It does not mean stop selling.** Volume is what an acquisition is bolted onto, and the FY27 target remains sensible as an operating goal. The question is only what wins when Vadim's time, Kyri's build queue or the reps' hours are contested.

**This is a recommendation to revisit, not a decision taken.** The ruling is Ethan's and he should have the number in front of him when he makes it.
